Lichen Zhu

M.S. Student, Electrical & Computer Engineering, Duke University
Research Intern, Center for Computational Evolutionary Intelligence

I work on multimodal large language models — specifically on how they store, compress, and retrieve information over long horizons. My recent projects study what personal LLM assistants forget when their conversation memory is compressed, and how video models select, or fail to select, the evidence a question actually depends on. I am interested in making these systems both more reliable and more efficient to run.

About

I am a Master's student in Electrical & Computer Engineering at Duke University, where I do research in the Center for Computational Evolutionary Intelligence (CEI), co-directed by Prof. Yiran Chen and Prof. Hai Li. I work closely with Yueqian Lin and Yudong Liu on multimodal large language models and efficient inference for vision-language models. Since June 2026 I have also been a research intern at Hefei University of Technology, working with Prof. Yanbin Hao and Dr. Shuo Wang on long-form video understanding.

Before Duke I received a B.Sc. in Financial Mathematics with First Class Honours from Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University and the University of Liverpool, where my dissertation applied stacked deep learning models to credit-risk prediction. That background still shapes how I work: I am drawn to problems where a careful diagnosis of what a model is actually doing has to come before a new method. I am always glad to hear about research or collaborations — get in touch.

Featured Research

  • HumanSys 2026

    Will My Assistant Remember My Allergy? What Personal LLM Assistants Forget When Conversation Memory Is Compressed

    Personal assistants compress conversation history to keep context costs down — and quietly drop the durable user preferences that matter most. We build a memory-compression framework that retains long-term preferences while still reducing storage cost.

  • Under Review · AAAI 2027

    Semantic-Unit Aggregation for Streaming Interaction Memory

    Streaming memory is usually chopped into fixed temporal windows, which cuts events in half. We organise interaction history by semantic unit instead, keeping original events intact and improving evidence retrieval over long horizons.

  • EMNLP 2026

    When No Answer Is Correct: Diagnosing Absent Answer Detection for MLLMs in Video Understanding

    When the correct answer simply isn't among the options, video MLLMs pick a plausible distractor rather than abstaining. We show this failure worsens under temporal reasoning and resists prompting-based fixes.
