About

I am a Master's student in Electrical & Computer Engineering at Duke University, working in the Center for Computational Evolutionary Intelligence with Yueqian Lin and Yudong Liu, under Prof. Yiran Chen and Prof. Hai Li.

My work is on multimodal large language models — how they store, compress, and retrieve information over long horizons. Recent projects ask what a personal assistant forgets once its conversation memory is compressed, and why video models fail to pick out the evidence a question actually depends on.

Before Duke I read Financial Mathematics at Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University and the University of Liverpool, where my dissertation applied stacked deep learning models to credit-risk prediction. Always glad to talk about research — get in touch.

HumanSys 2026

ACM Workshop on Human-Centered Systems, 2026

Details →

L. Zhu, Y. Lin, Y. Wang, Y. Liu, H. Li, Y. Chen

ACM Workshop on Human-Centered Systems (HumanSys), 2026 · co-located with ACM MobiCom 2026

Personal assistants compress conversation history to keep context costs down — and quietly drop the durable user preferences that matter most. We build a memory-compression framework that retains long-term preferences while still reducing storage cost.

EMNLP 2026

EMNLP, 2026

Details →

Y. Wang, Y. Lin, L. Zhu, Y. Liu, H. Li, Y. Chen

Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP), 2026

When the correct answer simply isn't among the options, video MLLMs pick a plausible distractor rather than abstaining. We show this failure worsens under temporal reasoning and resists prompting-based fixes.

L. Zhu, Y. Lin, Y. Wang, H. Li, Y. Chen

AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence, 2027

Streaming memory is usually chopped into fixed temporal windows, which cuts events in half. We organise interaction history by semantic unit instead, keeping original events intact and improving evidence retrieval over long horizons.

IEEE/CVF Winter Conference on Applications of Computer Vision (WACV), 2027

An information-bottleneck formulation of keyframe selection that maximises query-conditional mutual information and decomposes the combinatorial search into efficient frame-level scoring, improving long-form video QA under tight token budgets.
